15.1Eight hundred ninety-six experts

The text stack states it plainly: 896 routed experts with 16 active per token , 2 shared experts beside them , 93 layers deep . The awake share falls to 1.8% of routed experts — V3 woke 3.1%, Mixtral 25%. Each generation spends about the same per token and houses multiples more.

15.2What 2.8 trillion weighs

Artificial Analysis counts 2,800B trainable parameters for Kimi-K3 (max variant) with 104B active — cited here as their numbers, not the config's, since configs don't total themselves. The packing that makes 2.8T storable is in the config: experts quantize to MXFP4 , four bits per expert weight against the bf16 baseline of chapter 22. Narrow experts are a placement strategy as much as a quality trade: at full bf16 the resident set would need roughly four times the memory, and no fleet has it to spare.

Placement is the rest of the chapter's subject in miniature. Every token can reach any of 896 experts, so all 896 must be resident on some GPU the token's path can reach — offloading an expert means a transfer on the critical path whenever routing picks it. Wide expert parallelism spreads experts across ranks with all-to-all dispatch; the all-to-all's pace sets the step. Chapter 29 and the Two Orchestrators course take the fleet view; the config-level fact is simpler: 898 experts per MoE layer, 16 + 2 awake, everything resident.

15.4Check yourself

How many experts does one Kimi-K3 token activate per MoE layer?

  1. 896 — everything resident runs
  2. 18 — 16 routed plus 2 shared
  3. 16 — the shared experts are spares
  4. 8 — the V3 number
16 routed plus 2 always-on shared. Resident is 898; active is 18 — 1.8% of routed experts awake.

Why must all 896 experts stay resident even though 16 run?

  1. Quantization requires full residency
  2. The router picks the same 16 every time
  3. Any token can pick any expert, so an offloaded expert costs a transfer on the critical path
  4. Shared experts need the routed weights beside them
Routing follows content. The picks change per token, so every expert must be reachable without a fetch — placement, not just capacity.

Kimi-K3's topk_group is 1 against V3's 4-of-8 groups. What changed?

  1. Routing went back to flat top-16 across all 896 experts
  2. Experts are grouped 16 per group
  3. Only 1 expert is active per token
  4. Groups now hold the shared experts
One group containing everything is no constraint. At 896 experts the design bets on flat selection with scale doing the specializing.

The 2,800B / 104B figures in this chapter come from where?

  1. The pinned config's parameter keys
  2. The chapter's own arithmetic
  3. The Transformers modeling file
  4. Artificial Analysis charts, cited as their numbers
Configs don't total themselves. Third-party totals are cited as scores with the publisher named — never as config facts.

From chapter 12: V3 wakes 8 of 256 routed experts (3.1%). Kimi-K3 wakes 16 of 896 — what share?

  2. About 1.8% — finer still, at roughly flat per-token spend
  3. 50% — half the pool runs

16 / 896 ≈ 1.8%. Each generation wakes a smaller share while spending about the same per token — capacity multiplies, cost doesn't.
