Mixtral woke 2 of 8 big experts. DeepSeek-V3 wakes 8 of 256 small ones, routes them in groups, and keeps one shared expert on for every token — and Kimi-K3 triples the design again to 896. This chapter runs Mixtral's coarse block against V3's fine-grained one: smaller experts, group-constrained routing, and the first 3 layers left dense.

The 2-minute version

256 small experts (44M each) with 8 active per token in ≤4 of 8 groups, one shared expert always on, sigmoid scoring, no auxiliary loss, first 3 layers dense. Same per-token spend as coarse MoE (~396M vs ~394M per layer) for 32x the expert count.

After this chapter you can: compute active MLP with shared experts · explain group-constrained routing · say why no flagship routes from layer zero.

12.1Two hundred fifty-six small experts

The config states the scale: 256 routed experts , 8 active per token , each with an intermediate width of 2,048 against a hidden width of 7,168 . One expert holds 3 × 7,168 × 2,048 ≈ 44.0M weights — one quarter of a Mixtral expert — and a token visits eight plus one shared: about 396.4M of MLP per MoE layer. Routing 256 experts needs constraint, so V3 groups them in 8 and picks across at most 4 groups , with auxiliary-loss-free balancing .

12.2The shared expert beside them

Beside the 256 routed experts sits one shared expert that every token passes through . The module builds it as a dense MLP sized by the shared count, routes tokens to the picked experts, and adds both paths:

The router itself only emits logits ; grouping, sigmoid scoring and the 2.5 scaling factor shape the picks downstream. Kimi-K2 keeps the shared expert and the no-auxiliary-loss routing but drops the groups to 1 — 384 experts picked flat. V2-Lite, the earlier design, runs 2 shared experts with plain greedy softmax routing .

12.3Dense first, then route

The first 3 of 61 layers are dense : every token passes through full feed-forwards before any routing happens. Early layers learn the general representations every later expert builds on, and dense layers can't collapse into imbalance. GLM-4.5 makes the same call at 3 of 92 ; Kimi-K3 at 1 of 93. The fraction shrinks as the model grows, but no flagship routes from layer zero.

12.6Check yourself

About how many MLP weights does one DeepSeek-V3 token activate per MoE layer?

  1. 44.0M — one expert
  2. 396.4M — 8 routed plus 1 shared expert of 44.0M each
  3. 1.4B — all 256 routed experts at reduced strength
  4. 352.3M — the Mixtral number
9 × 3 × 7,168 × 2,048 = 396,361,728. Near Mixtral's 394.3M per layer — the finer design spends the same per token and buys 32x the expert count.

What do V3's 8 groups with at most 4 constrain?

  1. Which layers may contain experts
  2. How many tokens each expert receives
  3. How spread the 8 picks may be: they must come from no more than 4 of 8 expert groups
  4. The precision of expert weights
Group-constrained routing keeps picks placeable on hardware: experts that fire together can live together. Kimi-K2 later drops the constraint back to flat top-8.

Why are V3's first 3 layers dense?

  1. Early layers learn shared representations and dense layers can't collapse into imbalance
  2. Routers don't fit in early layers' memory budget
  3. Attention requires dense feed-forwards beneath it
  4. The first layers run on CPU
Routing from layer zero risks early collapse: experts that win early get all the gradient. Three dense layers buy a stable base every expert shares.

V2-Lite runs 2 shared experts with greedy softmax routing. What does that say about the shared count?

  1. Shared experts must always be exactly 1
  2. Greedy routing requires 2 shared experts
  3. Shared experts replace the router
  4. The shared count is a design choice: 2 in V2-Lite, 1 in V3, Kimi-K2 and GLM-4.5
n_shared_experts is 2 in V2-Lite's config and 1 in its successors. More shared capacity means less pressure on routing — at the cost of always-on arithmetic.

From chapter 11: Mixtral's router keeps top-2 weights and renormalizes them. Why?

  1. To balance load across experts
  2. To add jitter during inference
  3. So the kept weights sum to 1 and the output stays on scale
  4. To select which layers route
The dropped experts took probability mass with them. V3 renormalizes its top-8 the same way (norm_topk_prob) — dropping mass must never shrink outputs.
Takeaways
  • Fine-grained MoE: 256 small experts, top-8 in ≤4 groups, one shared, sigmoid × 2.5, no auxiliary loss.
  • Same per-token spend as coarse MoE (~396M vs ~394M per layer) buys 32x the expert count.
  • Dense-first layers stabilize routing; no flagship routes from layer zero.
