Positions told each token where it stands. Now the feed-forward splits in eight. Mixtral-8x7B keeps Mistral-7B's attention untouched and replaces every MLP with eight of them, waking two per token. The checkpoint holds 46.7B weights; one token touches about 12.9B. This chapter runs one token through the dense block on channel A and the routed block on channel B.

11.1Eight experts, two awake

The dense side runs its one MLP unconditionally. The MoE side scores all eight experts, keeps the top two, runs exactly those, and returns their weighted sum. Everything else — attention, norms, residuals — is identical, which is why Mixtral reads as Mistral with a choice bolted on.

11.3Active against resident

Active weights decide cost per token; resident weights decide the GPU. Per layer the MoE block touches 41.9M of attention plus 2 × 176.2M of experts: 394.3M, against the dense block's 218.1M from chapter 1. Across 32 layers plus 262M of untied embeddings, one token activates about 12.9B of the 46.7B resident. The shard headers at the pinned revision total 93.41 GB — 46.7B resident weights at 2 bytes each, recorded in capstones/results/weigh.json. The ratio — roughly one quarter awake — is the whole pitch: 47B-class capacity at 13B-class cost per token, provided the router spreads its picks.

That proviso is load balance. If every token picks experts 2 and 5, two experts train on everything and six train on nothing, while serving still pays all eight residents. Mixtral trains with jitter on the hidden states to shake routing up; later designs add explicit balance losses, bias corrections and group constraints. Chapters 12 and 13 show the machinery; chapter 15 shows it at 896 experts.

Easy to get wrong

Top-2 does not mean each expert sees half the tokens. Routing follows content: code tokens and prose tokens can pile onto different experts, and a benchmark that only serves one domain exercises a slice of the model. Measure routing on your own traffic before sizing experts.

11.4Build it

Write a top-k router over eight experts in pure Python: pick the best two scores, renormalize their weights, combine two expert outputs, and measure balance over a batch. The tests use fixed scores with no ties, check the weighted sum by hand, and catch the classic mistake of forgetting to renormalize after the top-k.

11.6Check yourself

Mixtral-8x7B routes one token with top-2 of 8. What runs, and what stays resident but idle?

  1. 2 experts run; the other 6 are offloaded to CPU
  2. 2 experts run; all 8 stay resident in GPU memory
  3. All 8 run at one-eighth strength each
  4. 1 shared expert runs; the router picks nothing
Active is a compute fact, resident is a memory fact. Every token could pick any expert, so all eight must be loaded. Mixtral has no shared expert — that is chapter 13.

About how many weights does one Mixtral token activate, model-wide?

  1. 46.7B — everything resident
  2. 7.2B — the Mistral-7B baseline
  3. About 12.9B — attention plus 2 experts per layer, plus embeddings
  4. About 1.4B — one layer's worth
394.3M per layer × 32 layers ≈ 12.6B, plus 262M of untied embeddings. The 46.7B answer confuses resident with active.

Why does the block renormalize the top-2 weights after the top-k?

  1. So the two kept weights sum to 1 and the output stays on the trained scale
  2. So every expert gets exactly half the tokens
  3. To add jitter during inference
  4. To keep the router's softmax from saturating
The dropped six experts took probability mass with them. Without renormalization the weighted sum would shrink every step. It does nothing for balance — that needs training-time machinery.

When does the jitter branch in MixtralSparseMoeBlock run?

  1. Every decode step, to spread load
  2. Whenever two experts tie
  3. When the router's entropy is low
  4. Only in training, when jitter noise is positive
The branch is guarded by self.training. Served routing is deterministic given the weights — which is exactly why serving inherits whatever imbalance training left behind.
Takeaways
  • Coarse MoE: one gate, top-2 of 8, renormalize, weighted sum. The only new math is the router.
  • 12.9B active against 46.7B resident: capacity at constant per-token cost, memory at full cost.
  • Routing follows content, so benchmarks exercise slices; balance is a training problem with serving consequences.
  • Fine-grained MoE multiplies the experts and shrinks them — chapter 12.
