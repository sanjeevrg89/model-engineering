Glossary

The terms the course uses, what each design calls them, and the chapter that explains them properly. Numbers are for the course's roster configs at the pinned revisions.

Token
The piece of text a model reads: a fragment a tokenizer splits words into, stored as an integer id. Vocabulary size is the number of possible ids (32,000 in Mistral-7B, 152,064 in Qwen2.5-7B). Chapter 2
Hidden vector
The list of numbers standing in for a token inside the model — hidden_size entries (3,584 in Qwen2.5-7B, 4,096 in Mistral-7B). Every block rewrites each token's vector before passing it on. Chapter 1
Weights
The learned matrices of the model, counted in parameters. One Qwen2.5-7B block has about 233M of them; the LM head scores the vocabulary. Chapter 1
Attention
A learned lookup: the current position builds a query, every earlier position offers a key and a value, and matching scores decide how much of each value blends in. Several heads run in parallel with different patterns. Chapter 1 · Zoo
Logits
The last block's output: one score per vocabulary entry. The sampler turns them into a next token. Qwen2.5-7B produces 152,064 per generated token. Chapter 1
Decoder block
One residual unit of a decoder-only model: RMSNorm, attention, RMSNorm, feed-forward. Every roster model stacks 27–92 of them. Chapter 1
Grouped-query attention (GQA)
Several query heads share one key/value head. Qwen2.5-7B shares 28 queries over 4 KV heads; Mistral-7B shares 32 over 8. Transformers repeats the shared heads verbatim with repeat_kv. Chapter 1 · Chapter 6
Sliding-window attention
Each token attends only to the last W tokens. Mistral-7B's window is 4,096; gpt-oss-120B's is 128; Qwen2.5-7B sets 131,072 and mostly disables it. Chapter 7
Multi-head latent attention (MLA)
Keys and values compress into one small latent per token (rank 512 in the roster's MLA models) plus a decoupled RoPE key. DeepSeek-V2-Lite debuts it; Kimi-K2 scales it. Chapter 8
DeepSeek sparse attention (DSA)
A lightning indexer scores every past token and full attention runs only over the top 2,048 (index_topk). Debuts in DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp on top of MLA. Chapter 9
Native sparse attention (NSA)
DSA's concurrent sibling: compressed coarse tokens, selected fine blocks and a sliding window per query, trained end to end. Chapter 9 · Zoo
Mixture of experts (MoE)
A router sends each token to a few expert feed-forwards: top-2 of 8 in Mixtral, top-8 of 256 in DeepSeek-V3, top-4 of 128 in gpt-oss-120B, top-8 of 384 in Kimi-K2. Chapter 11 · Chapter 12
Shared expert
One expert every token passes through, with the router topping it up from routed experts. The DeepSeekMoE idea, carried into V2-Lite, V3 and Kimi-K2. Chapter 13
RoPE
Rotary position embeddings: positions rotate queries and keys. Theta runs from 10,000 (Mistral-7B, DeepSeek-V3) to 5,000,000 (MiniMax-M2.5). Chapter 3
SwiGLU
The gated feed-forward in every roster model: gate and up projections multiplied, then down. Three matrices per layer, two per expert. Chapter 1 · Chapter 4
QK-norm
RMSNorm applied to queries and keys per head before attention. Qwen3-8B normalizes on the head dim; it stabilizes training at depth. Chapter 4
Hybrid attention
Mostly recurrent linear layers with periodic full-attention layers: 3:1 Gated DeltaNet in Qwen3-Next, KDA with NoPE MLA in Kimi Linear, Lightning with MLA in MiniMax-M2.5. Chapter 17 · Chapter 19
Multi-token prediction (MTP)
Training extra heads to predict several future tokens, which also yields draft heads for speculative decoding. DeepSeek-V3 trains with it. Chapter 21
Speculative decoding
Draft cheaply, verify once with the target: separate drafters, Medusa/EAGLE draft heads, or DSpark blocks. Yield is a formula in acceptance rate. Chapter 21 · Zoo
Diffusion language model
Generation by iterative denoising of masked tokens with bidirectional attention, instead of left-to-right prediction. LLaDA-8B trains this way from scratch; Dream-7B starts from autoregressive weights. No KV cache to reuse. Chapter 25 · Chapter 26
KV cache
The keys and values past tokens leave behind: 57,344 B per token for Qwen2.5-7B, 131,072 B for Mistral-7B and Mixtral-8x7B in bf16. MLA and hybrid models keep latents or fixed states instead. Chapter 1 · Chapter 20
Active parameters
The weights one token actually touches: the full block for dense models, the router's picks for MoE (about 13B of Mixtral's 47B, 37B of DeepSeek-V3's 671B). Resident weights still need their memory. Chapter 12
Floating-point format (e4m3, e5m2, e2m1)
A float's bits split into one sign, an exponent (how large the number can be) and a mantissa (how many meaningful digits survive). The letters after E name each count: e4m3 is 1 sign, 4 exponent, 3 mantissa; MLP modeling code calls IEEE FP16 float16_e5m10 and bf16 float16_e8m7. Chapter 22
bf16 vs FP16
Two ways to spend 16 bits: bf16 keeps fp32's 8-bit exponent, so no range surprises at training time; IEEE FP16 keeps 10 mantissa digits but a 5-bit exponent. Every roster config records bfloat16; FP16 survives as a serving dtype. Chapter 22
Quantization and block scale
Round each weight to the nearest value a narrow format can express, and store one accurate scale per block so small numbers lift back up. Classes: FP8 (8-bit values, 128-wide blocks in GLM-5.3, 32 in V4.1-Flash), MXFP4 (4-bit values, 32-wide scale blocks, gpt-oss and Kimi-K3 experts), NVFP4 (4-bit values, 16-wide FP8 scales under one FP32 global scale, Blackwell kernels).
